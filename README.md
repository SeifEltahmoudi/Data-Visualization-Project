# Data Visualization Project

## Overview

This project explores the factors that influence national happiness across countries between 2012 and 2017. Using data visualization techniques, the analysis examines how variables such as Human Development Index (HDI), income per person, corruption, democracy, and social indicators relate to happiness levels around the world.

The project was completed as part of the **DSCI 2411 – Data Visualization** course at **The American University in Cairo (AUC)**.

My role in the project focused on **Objective 2: Identifying Relationships Between Attributes**, where I developed Tableau visualizations to investigate which factors show the strongest association with national happiness. Through this analysis, I explored relationships between happiness and key socioeconomic indicators, identifying patterns, trends, and correlations within the data.

The findings were documented in a final report and presented to the class through a data-driven presentation.

## Objective 2: Identifying Relationships Between Attributes

This part of the project focuses on exploring the relationships between happiness and several national-level indicators through Tableau visualizations.

### Visualizations

- Relationship between HDI and Happiness Score
- Impact of Income per Person on Happiness
- Correlation between Corruption Perception Index (CPI) and Happiness
- Multivariate Analysis of Happiness using HDI and Income
- Average Happiness Across Different Democracy Levels

---

## Tools Used

- **Tableau** – Creating visualizations and analyzing relationships between happiness and socioeconomic indicators.
- **Data Storytelling** – Communicating insights and findings through a structured presentation.

---

## Dataset

The dataset combines multiple global indicators, including:

- Happiness Score (World Happiness Report)
- Human Development Index (HDI)
- Income per Person
- Corruption Perception Index (CPI)
- Democracy Score
- Population
- Murder Rate
- Suicide Rate

---

## Key Findings

- HDI showed the strongest relationship with national happiness.
- Income per person was positively associated with happiness, although with diminishing returns at higher income levels.
- Lower levels of perceived corruption were generally linked to higher happiness scores.
- Democracy showed a more complex relationship with happiness than other variables.
- National happiness is influenced by multiple socioeconomic and institutional factors rather than a single indicator.

---

## Conclusion

The analysis demonstrates that national happiness is strongly associated with human development, economic well-being, institutional quality, and social stability. While income contributes significantly to happiness, broader factors such as education, healthcare, governance, and public trust play an equally important role in shaping quality of life across nations.
