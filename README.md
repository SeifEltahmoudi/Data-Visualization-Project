# Data-Visualization-Project
Overview
This project explores the factors that influence national happiness across countries between 2012 and 2017. Using data visualization techniques, the analysis examines how variables such as Human Development Index (HDI), income per person, corruption, democracy, and social indicators relate to happiness levels around the world.
The project was completed as part of the DSCI 2411 – Data Visualization course at The American University in Cairo (AUC). My role in the project focused on Objective 2: Identifying Relationships Between Attributes, where I investigated which factors show the strongest association with national happiness. All visualizations were developed using Tableau to identify patterns, trends, and relationships within the data. The findings were documented in a final report and presented to the class through a data-driven presentation.

Conclusion
The analysis demonstrates that national happiness is strongly associated with human development, economic well-being, institutional quality, and social stability. While income contributes significantly to happiness, broader factors such as education, healthcare, governance, and public trust play an equally important role in shaping quality of life across nations.
